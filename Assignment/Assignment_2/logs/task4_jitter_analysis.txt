ASSIGNMENT-02 Task 4 - Real-Time Scheduling & Jitter Analysis
Date: 2026-11-06

Measurement setup
-----------------
- Target: ARMv7 Linux 5.15.0 guest in QEMU vexpress-a9 (TCG virtualization),
  256 MiB RAM, one emulated Cortex-A9 CPU.
- Jitter is recorded in app/sensor_thread.c by the Sensor Thread. Each sample
  stores the absolute CLOCK_MONOTONIC deadline passed to
  clock_nanosleep(CLOCK_MONOTONIC, TIMER_ABSTIME, ...) and the monotonic time
  read immediately after a successful wake-up. Jitter is wakeup - deadline.
- The test used -i 10 -d 40 and SMS_JITTER_CSV. The optional instrumentation
  collected the first 1,000 wake-ups per run; normal runs without that
  environment variable do not write or collect jitter samples.
- Because sensor reads and processing can overrun a 10 ms period under QEMU,
  the measured path skips expired release deadlines before sleeping for the
  next future absolute deadline. This prevents a late read from being counted
  repeatedly as subsequent sleep-wakeup jitter. The number of skipped
  deadlines is reported separately. Raw deadline increments are therefore
  multiples of 10 ms, not necessarily exactly 10 ms.
- Both policy runs exited successfully, recorded exactly 1,000 rows, and had
  no early wake-ups. Independent checks verified every jitter_ns value equals
  wakeup_ns - deadline_ns and the deadline increments are positive multiples
  of 10 ms.
- The guest reported memory locking enabled. It confirmed SCHED_FIFO priority
  50 for one run and SCHED_OTHER priority 0 for the comparison run.

Across each full 40-second app run, the Sensor Thread reported 2,742 overdue
deadlines skipped in the FIFO run and 2,454 in the OTHER run. The raw CSV is
limited to the first 1,000 wake-ups, whereas these skip counts cover the full
run. The figures and deadline gaps show that the 10 ms work period was
frequently overrun in the emulated workload. FIFO did not materially reduce
the measured average jitter in this run; its P99 was about 141 us lower, while
its minimum was higher. The data does not support a claim that FIFO
consistently outperformed OTHER.

-----------------------------
- The direct-measurement QEMU run loaded the driver and ran the complete app
  for 40 seconds under each policy. Both returned exit status 0, processed
  sensor samples, generated alerts, and reported zero circular-buffer
  overflows. See logs/task4_direct_sensor_qemu.txt and the raw CSV files.
- After the instrumentation change, the ordinary default-mode QEMU integration

- `make -C app host arm test` passed after the change.

Interpretation and limitations
------------------------------
- The deadline is absolute and uses CLOCK_MONOTONIC; the wake-up timestamp is
  taken before the sensor read and application processing. Expired deadlines
  are skipped so this statistic measures lateness after the selected future
  sleep deadline, not the duration of a sample's processing.
- The guest kernel previously measured CLOCK_MONOTONIC resolution as 10 ms,
  with CONFIG_HZ=100, CONFIG_PREEMPT_NONE=y, and no CONFIG_HIGH_RES_TIMERS.
  Timer granularity is a major limit on interpretation, especially for values
  below one tick.
- QEMU TCG adds host scheduling and virtualization effects. Guest SCHED_FIFO
  does not make the host QEMU process real-time. These measurements do not
  establish hard real-time guarantees or predict physical ARM hardware.
- FIFO and OTHER were run sequentially, not in randomized repeated order, and
  no controlled competing-load sweep was performed. Host/QEMU load can affect
  the results. The Sensor Thread blocks in absolute nanosleep rather than
  busy-spinning.
- High skipped-deadline counts indicate that the app's read/processing work
  could not sustain the requested 10 ms cadence in this emulated run. This is
  separate from the recorded wake-up jitter and should be considered when
  interpreting the comparison.
